Orbita Agent Architecture

Orbita is a CRM for B2B sales teams with an agent inside. A seller adds a sales conversation: a meeting transcript or a WhatsApp chat. The agent finds the right opportunity, proposes updates to 22 CRM fields, and backs every value with the line where it was said. The seller decides what is written.

1 · Architecture type

What kind of AI system is this?

AI products sit on a ladder. What separates the rungs is who decides the next step.

AssistantYou ask, it answers, nothing else happens. Explore works this way: a seller asks about the pipeline and Claude answers from the records that seller can read.
Agentic workflowAn event starts it, code runs the steps in a fixed order, and Claude does one of them. It acts for the seller, within permissions the seller sets.
Autonomous agentClaude picks its own next step and tools, in a loop. Updating a system of record follows the same steps every time, so fixed steps are easier to test, cheaper and easier to audit.

One sentence for reviewersOrbita is an agentic workflow: one Claude step inside a deterministic pipeline, acting on the CRM through per-field permissions, with a human in the loop. Explore is the assistant layer.

2 · The loop

One conversation, start to finish

Each lane is who does the work. Eight of the nine steps are code or the seller; the model runs once.

  1. SellerAdds a conversation.Drops a file or pastes text: Teams or Zoom captions (.vtt), .srt, Otter, Granola, plain "Name: text", or a WhatsApp export.
  2. CodeReads it.Every format becomes numbered lines with a speaker. Names from the sales team are marked as the seller's side.
  3. CodeFinds the opportunity.Contacts, the company in the title, email domains and mentions point to one open opportunity. If none fits it proposes a new client and opportunity; if two fit, the seller chooses.
  4. CodeRuns the pre-checks.The seller must be allowed to read the opportunity, a recording cut mid-sentence is blocked, and creating an opportunity must be allowed.
  5. ClaudeReads the conversation once.It gets the agent's rules and the current CRM values, and returns only the fields it found evidence for, each with a quoted line.
  6. CodeValidates the answer.Each quote must be on the line it cites; dates, numbers and departments must be in format; the stage moves one step forward at most; an economic buyer or champion must be a named person on the customer side.
  7. CodeApplies the permissions.Always allow writes at once, with Undo. Needs approval waits. Never skips the field. A value that contradicts one the seller approved always waits.
  8. SellerReviews and submits.Approves, edits or rejects each waiting field.
  9. Code · writeOne function writes to the CRM.It keeps the old and new value, who decided and when, in the activity log.
3 · The model step

What Claude receives, and what it returns

Each call has two parts. The first never changes: the agent's policy, the definitions of the 22 fields, the stage rules and two worked examples. It is cached, so from the second call it is read at a tenth of the input price. The second part is this conversation: the current CRM values and the numbered transcript.

The 22 fields come in six groups: Conversation (3), Next step (3), Qualification in MEDDPICC (8), Scope and budget (5), Company profile (2) and Stage (1). The deal amount, close date, probability, discount and owner belong to the seller: the agent never proposes them, and the code drops them if it tries.

4 · Human boundary

Where humans stay in control

The seller sets, field by field, how much the agent may do on its own, in three levels.

LevelWhat happensDefault for
Always allowWritten at once, with UndoSummary, Objections, Commitments
Needs approvalWaits for the seller's decisionEvery other field, and creating a new opportunity
NeverNot proposed and not writtenChosen by the seller, field by field
Contradictions always waitIf the conversation contradicts a value the seller approved, no setting writes over it.
One way to writeEvery change goes through the same function, which records who decided: the seller or a permission.
Access before readingA seller can't run the agent on another seller's opportunity; the attempt is logged and nothing is shown.
5 · Data

The data, today and next

All data is synthetic: 14 client companies, 16 opportunities across six stages from New Lead to Contract, a six-person team with access rules, and 11 test conversations in six formats, including a WhatsApp chat. Today the records live in the repository as JSON, and each browser keeps its own working copy.

6 · Evaluation

How we know it works

The evaluation follows the evals-skills method: audit the eval, read real traces, name the failure modes in plain words, then turn each one into a check. Eleven test conversations cover the complete discovery call and the edge cases: a recording cut mid-sentence, another seller's opportunity, voices the notetaker could not name, a deal amount said out loud, terms agreed on WhatsApp.

  • Two of the eleven cases are stopped by code before the model, which is why a full run costs 9 calls.
  • The first live run found a bug in the eval itself: the expected answer still used the old term decision-maker after the field was renamed Economic buyer. The eval was fixed, not the model.
  • 49 adversarial tests each feed the code one deliberate defect, such as an invented quote, a stage jump or another seller's opportunity, and check that it is caught.
  • 536 checks of the prototype's screens at two screen widths, and 12 checks of the full loop through the local server.
  • Through the page's server, the Acme call was read in 21 seconds for USD 0.07, with all 22 fields backed by a quote.
  • Next: the domain expert reviews 15 traces in an error-discovery tool and writes what is wrong in plain words. The notes become failure modes, fixes and new test cases.
